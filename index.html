What I do

I run a private setup of several machines and handle all of it myself: services that restart when they crash, scheduled jobs, monitoring that alerts me when something breaks, encrypted backups, and AI models running on my own hardware. Most days it runs without me touching it.

At work I've built internal tools: intranet pages, CRM pages, and small utilities that cut out repetitive manual steps. The hard part is usually not the code. It's understanding the job well enough that people can use the tool without training.

How I work

I don't call something working until I've tested it, ideally with a test written to break it. If a service is supposed to come back after a reboot, I reboot the machine and check.

I use AI tools a lot, and I'm open about it. They get me from not knowing something to a working version I can test much faster. They don't decide what gets built, and when the result is wrong, that's on me.

Strengths & limits

Getting data into a usable report. If you can describe the report, I can build it. I work out what question it needs to answer, find where the data lives, pull it out of whatever format it's stuck in, and set it up to run on a schedule so nobody has to rebuild it by hand. If a computer can open or export a file, I can usually get the data out. When no tool exists I write one, and I prefer open source.

For example, on my own systems I've parsed, sorted, and indexed tens of thousands of records. In my current job I built internal pages and tools to take manual steps out of the quoting process.

Running AI models privately. I set up and run open-weight language models on hardware I own. That includes serving them, sending each request to the right model based on cost and ability, connecting them to private documents so answers come from your data, and limiting what they're allowed to do. It all works offline, which matters for organisations that can't send their data to an outside AI service.

Where I stop. I build on top of models; I don't train them. No fine-tuning, model architecture, or statistical modelling. My production experience is my own environment plus internal tools at past jobs, not a large team running a large company's systems. Better you know that up front.

Range. Over about twenty years I've done broadcast video and audio production on deadline, photography and graphics, WordPress and hand-written websites, intranets and CRM spaces on SharePoint and Salesforce, and now Linux, networking, containers, and automation. I went from no infrastructure background to running a multi-machine setup on my own in about six months.

The common thread is that I take something complicated, get it working myself, and then explain it to the people who have to use it. I'm not the deepest specialist in any one of these areas, and if you need ten years of nothing but Kubernetes, someone else is a better fit. I'm most useful on small teams, on internal tools, and on the work that falls between departments and doesn't have an owner.
